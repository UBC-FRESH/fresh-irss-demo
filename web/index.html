UBC-FRESH · IRSS × Silva21 expansion

Environmental intelligence for forest decisions.

IRSS and Silva21 built two decades of measurement science. FRESH built the decision engines. This demo shows the seam between them: eight small, reviewable integrations that move climate, disturbance, habitat, carbon, and operational evidence into a forest estate model — without pretending to have produced new science.

The finding

Strong engines. A thin environmental state layer.

FRESH can optimise an estate, schedule operations, and fit distributions. What it mostly lacks is a shared, provenance-bearing description of the forest state those engines act on. IRSS and Silva21 supply exactly that layer — if it is wired in as contracts and adapters rather than ported as papers.

01

Measure, then contract

LiDAR, optical time series, and climate products already exist. The integration task is a small schema, a reader, and an acceptance test — not a new remote-sensing model.

02

Keep provenance visible

A number without a source, a version, and an applicability boundary is not decision evidence. Model cards and report sidecars travel with the value.

03

Refuse the wrong work

Urban energy mapping, global biodiversity SDMs, and a from-scratch fire simulator are screened out. The programme stays credible by refusing work that does not change a FRESH decision.

Inventory & LiDAR

LiDAR becomes inventory — with a model card

FEMIC's LiDAR bundle now feeds an inventory.als table: a published, basal-area-only pilot with per-row model identity and error. The EFI model card carries training data, accuracy, and applicability alongside the sensor product.

Why it matters: a canopy product becomes a defensible inventory input, and the model's limits travel with the number into curve selection.

Boundary: basal area only, 14.9% TSA 23 coverage, RMSE 8.86 m²/ha on 36 in-range plots. Height failed independent validation; volume remains research-only. The release is a pilot, not an operational inventory.

Climate

One climate table, with its provenance attached

femic.climate.v1 fixes the record shape: AOI, variable, canonical unit, year, scenario, GCM, value, variance, source, downscaling method, and retrieval time. A ClimateNA/SilvR21 adapter writes into it.

Why it matters: every climate-sensitive method — growth modifiers, mortality, habitat, risk — can read the same table instead of growing three incompatible adapters.

Boundary: this is a data contract and validator, not a climate model, a GCM ensemble selector, or a claim about future climate. The sample on this page is synthetic.

Complete

Disturbance

One event record for fire, insect, drought, wind, and harvest

disturbance.v1 carries type, year, extent, severity, attribution, confidence, source, and provenance. FEMIC writes it; ws4 reads it and reports the event in a scenario. A post-fire recovery layer is also in place.

Why it matters: a single event can move from data to model to operations without every repository inventing its own event table.

Boundary: a schema and one consumer, not a near-real-time alert. The static DHI/recovery layers are not alert thresholds; the failed NSR case-control test is not an operational detector.

Habitat

Biodiversity becomes a landscape account

Dynamic Habitat Indices are ingested as a static productivity surrogate and reported as an account and web layer. The exact Coops et al. (2016) structure habitat index adds a LiDAR-derived, stand- and landscape-level view with coverage diagnostics and a DHI comparison.

Why it matters: habitat stops being an external map and becomes a value that a schedule can be compared against, tied to structure that management actually changes.

Boundary: DHI is a coarse productivity surrogate, not a species-specific habitat model. The structure index needs its exact metrics; the TFL 6 bundle reports missing metrics, and disagreement with DHI is reported, not hidden.

Carbon

A shared carbon account, not a fourth carbon model

CARB-01 defines the account fields — stand biomass, harvested biomass, product pool, emissions, and decay — and emits them from a ws4 scenario, reusing the ws3 CBM path for product and decay boundaries. Every conversion factor is labelled.

Why it matters: carbon becomes a comparable account across ws3, ws4, and FEMIC instead of a separate model with its own silent boundary choices.

Boundary: v0 may convert volume with a declared basic density and carbon fraction; the biomass curve family is a later refinement. Stand, product, and soil boundaries are not interchangeable, and false precision is a known risk.

Operations

Dead wood is not valued like green wood

The first CARB-03 slice, in an open FHOPS pull request, adds a shared salvage.decay.v1 contract, the published Prestemon & Holmes (2008) retention curve, Barrette et al. (2015) product windows, and fhops report salvage-shelf-life. Commercial thinning prioritisation from drone LiDAR is shortlisted for the next wave.

Why it matters: salvage timing and shelf life become visible to operations and carbon valuation, where dead wood was previously valued as if it were green.

Boundary: the retention curve is a US Pacific Northwest mixed-conifer fire-killed reference, not a BC-specific, species-specific, or roadside-storage operational curve. Grade/value loss and scheduling remain follow-on work.

Provenance

Decision-makers can see where a number came from

A ws4.report.provenance.v1 sidecar records report names, bytes, SHA-256 digests, source metadata and hashes, run and dataset identity, and an explicit not_stated uncertainty status. The Reports tab shows a provenance badge and the forest-condition report cards reuse the same discipline.

Why it matters: provenance becomes legible at the point of decision, and missing uncertainty is stated instead of implied away.

Boundary: provenance is not validation. Uncertainty bands remain absent where inputs do not state them. Condition cards are review-required and advisory, not operational early warning.

Storyboards

A scenario becomes a narrative, not a table dump

A one-page Markdown storyboard combines indicators, uncertainty, and trade-offs from an existing run, reusing the condition card and provenance. Spatial resilience metrics — connectivity, age-class diversity, and fragmentation — are emitted as ws4 accounts with a two-run comparison.

Why it matters: planners and reviewers get a shareable account of what changed and what it cost, with the definitions and units attached.

Boundary: the storyboard is Markdown only; charts, maps, and browser storyboards remain separate. Visual bias and accessibility are open concerns, and the metrics are reported, not yet placed in the objective.

Public fixture

A small map with a loud provenance label

Seven clipped VRI blocks, one road job, and one landing in a ~100 ha TFL 6 pilot AOI. The geometry is public; the map is rendered from a 9 KB GeoJSON file; the operational numbers are synthetic. This is the shape of a case study, not a harvest plan.

What is real

  • LidarBC-derived DEM, DSM, CHM, slope, and canopy products.
  • Seven clipped VRI polygons and their block geometry.
  • Bundle, product, and selection hashes in provenance.json.
  • OGL-BC licence and source attribution.

What is synthetic

  • Machine rates, work volume (20 m³/ha), and productivity (500 m³/day).
  • Landing capacity and the road job.
  • Every operational conclusion a solver might draw from them.

Provenance & limits

What this page can and cannot support

This is a communication artifact built from public planning records. It is designed to make the integration seams legible, not to substitute for a validation report.

Sources

  • IRSS × Silva21 expansion mine — registry, theme map, first-wave briefs, readiness notes, and handoffs.
  • candidates.yaml — the machine-readable source of truth for status and scope.
  • Public target-repository evidence: FEMIC, ws4, Nemora, and FHOPS issue and pull request records.
  • FHOPS TFL 6 fixture — public LiDAR-derived products and clipped VRI geometry, OGL-BC.

Method

The mine reviewed public research pages, project pages, publication metadata, and abstracts — not full texts and not a systematic review. Readiness is an integration judgement, not a claim that the science is settled. Counts are a 2026-11-03 snapshot.

The map re-projects a 9 KB public GeoJSON fixture from EPSG:3005 to EPSG:4326 for browser display. No source raster, private path, credential, or raw inventory record is included.

Not claimed

  • No operational harvest, salvage, or thinning recommendation.
  • No field accuracy, calibrated cost, or production rate.
  • No species-specific habitat model or near-real-time alert.
  • No live climate projection and no GCM ensemble selection.
  • No claim that a merged contract is a validated model.

Accessibility & reuse

The page uses semantic landmarks, a skip link, visible focus, keyboard-operable map features, reduced-motion support, and responsive layouts tested from 320 px upward. Colour is never the only status signal.

Code is MIT-licensed. The TFL 6 fixture retains OGL-BC attribution and the FHOPS provenance record. Reuse the fixture only with that attribution and its stated boundaries.

What remains for a live version

Roadmap
  1. Public assistant deployment. A local opt-in same-origin service and cited-answer contract exist; a public host, per-client rate limits, and a human review path remain. Keep the static page’s disabled state as the default.
  2. Interactive map. Replace the fixture with a hosted, permissioned layer service and real DHI, recovery, and inventory layers. Keep the provenance badge and the “static overlay is not an alert” boundary.
  3. Live registry feed. Generate the cards and counts from the registry at build time so statuses cannot drift from the source of truth.
  4. Review and validation. Add a named reviewer, a dated evidence page per card, and a link to each acceptance test — not just the merge commit.
